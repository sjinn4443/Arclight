## Confirmed findings

1. **Infant refractive urgency is escalated twice.** `src/clinical-interpreter.js:78` sets infant high hypermetropia to soon, then lines 296–300 bump it again to Urgent today. Myopia has the same duplicated branch although it is absent from the normal Baby case picker. Use one explicit rule, not two layers of escalation.
2. **Infant abnormal-reflex urgency is inconsistent.** Reduced infant left reflex (`normal-dark`) and infant corneal opacity remain Refer soon while dull reflex/dense cataract use Urgent today. Reduced or abnormal infant reflex requires a coherent prompt for urgent specialist assessment via the local pathway. Do not infer an identical fixed timing for every infant condition from a UK guideline.
3. **Test mode loses onset/glare settings.** Browser reproduction: Sudden + Glare on becomes Gradual + Glare after starting and stopping a test. `src/test-mode.js:184` omits these fields from the snapshot and restoration reapplies case defaults. Restore the actual prior context.
4. **Test cases inherit unnormalised modifiers.** `startTestRound` snapshots and locks cataract, pupil, lid and motion controls without resetting them to the selected case baseline. These can obscure or contradict the expected recognition answer. Snapshot everything relevant, apply a clean case, then restore on exit. Baby mode also uses the full test pool rather than the filtered Baby catalogue. Source-confirmed; not every modifier combination was rendered.
5. **Snapshot restoration reapplies case changes after restoring generated state.** The refraction change dispatch calls `setRefraction` again, regenerating cortical patterns and clearing axis/offset state. Preserve exact prior generated case state after the last case-change event. This is particularly relevant to cortical patterns.
6. **Result label does not identify the teaching context.** The untouched main card reads Action: Reassuring. That is correct for the selected normal example but remains unchanged when the user imposes poor visibility or changes the simulated eyes. Use a concise Example action label, as in corrected Swollen Discs, rather than implying an assessment of the manipulated patient view.

## Shared engine and clinical scope

- Fundal Reflex draws anterior eyes, pupil apertures and fundal-reflex effects. It has no Holo/BIO retinal mode. Do not rotate this whole scene to copy a retinal-viewer fix.
- Internal left/right identifiers describe screen positions; the patient's RE is on screen left. The asymmetric case mapper intentionally uses this convention. Do not reverse its branches merely because their internal names look opposite to the patient labels.
- Retinoblastoma/leucocoria, vitreous haemorrhage, retinal detachment and angle closure retain urgent example actions. All 32 catalogue cases have rendered outputs in the sweep.
- Tier spread is broadly sensible: primary normal/technique/alignment/white reflex, intermediate refractive and anterior/media examples, advanced detailed lens/vitreoretinal pathology. The catalogue spans 32 cases and the bank has 68 questions. Existing bank checks pass, not independent clinical certification.
- The normal blue-reflex teaching should preserve the distinction from unexplained white reflex; no new colour-based diagnostic rule is proposed.

Clinical reference: NIPE handbook eye section, including abnormal red-reflex referral and local pathways: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/newborn-and-infant-physical-examination-programme-handbook/newborn-and-infant-physical-examination-screening-programme-handbook.
