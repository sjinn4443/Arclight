Dermoscopy

Melanoma-like: ABCDEFG

Two halves do not broadly match in shape or colour.

Edge not smooth or even: uneven, blurred, notched or ragged.

Several colours in one lesion, such as red, white, blue-grey or black.

Unusually dark or darker than the patient's other lesions.

New or changing in size, shape, colour, surface, symptoms or pain.

F: Funny-looking. Stands out from the patient's other moles or skin lesions.

G: Gives symptoms. New itch, bleeding, oozing or crusting without a clear cause.

BCC/SCC signs: JFK

Look for a shiny or translucent rim, or a sore that does not heal.

Look for tiny branching vessels on or around the lesion.

Consider SCC in a concerning hard, raised rough lump. Scale or tenderness alone is not enough.

Consider SCC when a lesion grows quickly, remains ulcerated or bleeds without a clear cause.

Chaos + Clues

Uneven colour or structure from side to side on dermoscopy.

If chaos: check clues

Grey, blue or white areas/lines, or black dots/clods at the lesion edge.

Off-centre blank-looking area, thick dark network or thick branched lines.

One-sided streaks or pseudopods at the edge.

Mixed vessel patterns or chaotic nail pigment.

Exception

Escalate adult change, pigmented nodule, grey head/neck or palm/sole ridge pigment.

Rash triage

Darker skin: check darkening, purple/grey change, warmth or swelling.

New, worsening or spreading rashes are more concerning than stable ones.

Pick the main surface pattern: blister/pustule, bump, flat patch, eczema or plaque.

Severe itch suggests active inflammation, infestation or urticaria.

On darker skin, inflammation may look purple-grey, darker or swollen.

Same day: rapid spread, severe pain, extensive blistering or fever/unwell. Emergency: airway swelling or breathing difficulty, severe mucosal/peeling illness or a non-blanching rash with serious illness.

Wood's lamp guide

Technique: use a blacked out room. Creams, deodorant, lint and surface products can fluoresce.

Blue-green - M. tinea capitis

Supports Microsporum tinea capitis; a negative lamp does not exclude fungus.

Yellow-orange - Pityriasis versicolor

Supports pityriasis versicolor if the scaly rash fits.

Coral-red - Erythrasma

Supports erythrasma in folds; fluorescence comes from Corynebacterium.

Bright blue-white - Vitiligo

Vitiligo often looks sharply brighter, showing edge and extent.

Orange-red - Acne porphyrins

May be seen with acne porphyrins. Use as a supportive sign, not a stand-alone diagnosis.

White - head lice nits

Nits may fluoresce; confirm attached eggs on hair shafts.
