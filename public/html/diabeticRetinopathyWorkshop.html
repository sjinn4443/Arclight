Pragmatic Diabetic Retinopathy Screening

This is a non-traditional, pragmatic diabetic eye screening strategy designed for general primary and secondary level health care settings.

It aims to integrate screening into general medical and non-communicable disease (NCD) clinics.

Mid-level eye care and some general health care workers can deliver this screening using the Arclight Package.

Opportunistic screening

Pragmatic Diabetic Retinopathy Screening in NCD Clinics

Bring diabetic eye screening into the clinic where people with diabetes are already receiving care.

01

The missed eye check

People with diabetes rarely have an eye examination in their routine medical care.

Image placeholder: routine diabetes clinic visit without an eye check

02

Put screening where patients already are

This opportunistic strategy places a trained and equipped mid-level eye care specialist in a general medical or NCD clinic.

Image placeholder: trained eye care worker using Arclight in an NCD clinic

03

A higher-risk clinic population

Patients attending these clinics are at higher risk of all forms of eye disease, including sight-threatening diabetic retinopathy.

Image placeholder: NCD clinic flow with high-risk diabetic patients highlighted

04

Actively screen during routine care

It therefore makes sense to actively and opportunistically screen for diabetic retinopathy and other eye diseases in these clinics.

Whole-eye screening

Screening Diabetic Retinopathy and Other Eye Diseases

Diabetic retinopathy matters, but it is not the only common cause of treatable sight loss in these patients.

01

Look beyond diabetic retinopathy

Alongside diabetic retinopathy, these patients commonly have:

  • Refractive error
  • Presbyopia
  • Cataract
  • Glaucoma

Image placeholders: specs, glaucoma, diabetic retinopathy

Image placeholder: specs

Image placeholder: glaucoma

Image placeholder: DR retina

02

Retina-only screening can miss treatable disease

If screening focuses only on retinopathy, many treatable causes of sight loss can be missed.

Image placeholder: narrow fundoscopy-only pathway missing other eye disease

03

Fundoscopy is essential, but not enough

Screening typically focuses on looking at the back of the eye by performing fundoscopy.

We will use the Arclight direct ophthalmoscope to examine the retina through a dilated pupil, but the screening encounter should include more than this.

Image placeholder: Arclight direct ophthalmoscope examining the dilated retina

04

Build a fuller screening sequence

  1. Take a history
  2. Test visual acuity
  3. Check pupil reactions to light
  4. Examine the front of the eye
  5. Perform the fundal red reflex
  6. Then dilate the pupils for fundoscopy

Image placeholder: front-of-eye Arclight loupe exam

Image placeholder: red reflex before dilation

05

Identify more treatable causes of sight loss

These additional examinations help identify patients with:

  • Refractive error
  • Presbyopia
  • Cataract
  • Glaucoma

Blood sugar and insulin

What is Diabetes?

Diabetes means the sugar level in the blood is too high for too long.

01

Food becomes glucose

Food, especially carbohydrates, is broken down into sugar called glucose.

Interactive graphic: food changing into glucose in the bloodstream

rice bread fruit
02

Insulin moves glucose into the body

Insulin is a hormone that helps move sugar from the blood into the body so it can be used for energy.

Food Glucose in blood Insulin opens the door Energy for the body

03

In diabetes, insulin is missing or not working

There is not enough insulin, or insulin does not work properly. Sugar stays in the blood instead of being used by the body.

04

High sugar damages the body

Over time, high blood sugar damages nerves and blood vessels all over the body, including the eyes.

Retina blood vessels

What is Diabetic Retinopathy?

Diabetic retinopathy is damage to the retina caused by high sugar levels in the blood.

01

The retina lets you see

The retina is the light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye that allows vision.

Interactive graphic: retina at the back of the eye with blood vessels

02

High sugar damages tiny vessels

High sugar damages the tiny blood vessels in the retina. These vessels can leak, bleed, or close off.

Interactive graphic: high sugar damages vessels, then they leak, bleed, or close off

03

New weak vessels can grow

Later, new weak vessels can grow. They bleed easily and can block the drainage of fluid in the eye.

Interactive graphic: new weak vessels grow, bleed, and block drainage

04

Symptoms often come late

At first, there are no symptoms. Over time, diabetic retinopathy can cause:

  • Gradual blurred central vision
  • Dark spots or shadows
  • Sudden painless complete loss of vision
  • High pressure with painful red eye and vision loss

Image placeholder: four symptom examples for diabetic retinopathy

Two main pathways

Vision Loss in Diabetes

Vision loss mainly happens when normal vessels leak or when new fragile vessels grow.

Interactive graph: two pathways to vision loss in diabetes

High blood sugar
A Leaking normal vessels Fluid + hard exudates -> maculopathy
B New fragile vessels Bleeding + scarring + high pressure
A

Normal blood vessels start to leak fluid

Fluid leaks into the retina, leaving yellow marks called hard exudates.

Interactive graphic: leaking retinal vessels with yellow hard exudates

A

Maculopathy blurs central vision

The macula is the central part of the retina. When it becomes swollen and fluid-filled, central vision becomes blurry.

This happens painlessly over weeks and months. Damage mainly affecting the macula is called maculopathy.

Interactive graphic: swollen macula causing blurred central vision

clear edge blurred centre clear edge
A

Maculopathy on direct ophthalmoscopy

This is what maculopathy looks like on direct ophthalmoscopy with an Arclight. The pupils are dilated and the media is clear.

Video placeholder: Maculopathy on Direct Ophthalmoscopy

B

New fragile blood vessels grow

These new vessels damage the eye in three main ways.

  • Bleeding onto the retina and into the vitreous
  • Scarring of the retina causing retinal detachment
  • Blocking fluid drainage causing rubeotic glaucoma
