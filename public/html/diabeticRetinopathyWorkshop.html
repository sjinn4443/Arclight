DIABETIC RETINOPATHY PROTOCOL

Protocol: 10 Steps, 3 Phases

A practical sequence for screening diabetic retinopathy and other important eye disease inside a medical NCD clinic.

A

Phase A: Steps 1-3

  1. Eye history
  2. Measure distance visual acuity with pinhole
  3. Test near vision for presbyopia
B

Phase B: Steps 4-6

  1. Examine pupils with the light of the loupe
  2. Look at the front of the eye with the loupe
  3. Dilate pupils
07

NCD medical consultation

While pupils dilate, diabetes, blood pressure and weight review continue in parallel.

Phase A

History, distance VA with pinhole, near vision

Phase B

Pupils, front of eye, dilate pupils

07 NCD medical consultation

Diabetes, BP, weight

Phase C

Fundal reflex, fundoscopy, action based on findings

C

Phase C: Steps 8-10

  1. Check fundal reflex
  2. Perform fundoscopy
  3. Action based on findings
Map

medical NCD clinic algorithm

The protocol should sit inside the medical NCD clinic workflow, not outside it.

Always refer if:

  • Proliferative signs (NVD, NVE, VH, PRH, TRD)
  • Maculopathy
  • Severe NPDR (4-2-1 rule)
  • Abnormal pupils (poor response, RAPD)
  • Lost or reduced fundal reflex
  • Iris neovascularization (rubeosis)
  • Unexplained poor vision (not improving with pinhole)
  • Suspected glaucoma (cupped disc, poor pupil)

Key principles:

  • Look for ALL causes of sight loss, not just diabetic retinopathy
  • Pragmatic, not perfect - focus on clear danger signs
  • Integrate into routine medical NCD clinic workflow
  • Simple, safe, and scalable for low-resource settings

4-2-1 Rule

This is how to recognise Severe Non-Proliferative Diabetic Retinopathy (NPDR) and vision-threatening diabetic retinopathy signs.

If there are haemorrhages/microaneurysms in all 4 retinal quadrants, venous beading in 2 or more quadrants or intraretinal microvascular abnormalities (IRMA) in 1 or more quadrants, treat this as severe NPDR and refer.

DIABETIC RETINOPATHY PROTOCOL

Final Decisions

Make the safest practical decision. If there is any doubt, refer to eye clinics.

!

Always refer if

  • Proliferative signs
  • Maculopathy
  • Abnormal pupil response
  • Lost fundal reflex
  • Unexplained poor vision not improving with pinhole
  • Suspected glaucoma: poor pupil response or cupped disc

Key messages

  1. Integrate the eye exam into the medical NCD clinic workflow
  2. Avoid silo thinking: pragmatic, scalable, safe
  3. Look for all causes of sight loss
  4. Focus on clear danger signs
  5. Refer to eye clinics if any doubt

Introduction to Pragmatic Diabetic Retinopathy Screening

A practical diabetic eye screening strategy for primary and secondary level health care settings.

01 This is a non-traditional, pragmatic diabetic eye screening strategy designed for general primary and secondary level health care settings.

02 It aims to integrate screening into general medical and non-communicable disease (NCD) clinics.

03 Mid-level eye care and some general health care workers can deliver this screening using the Arclight Package.

INTRODUCTION

Pragmatic Diabetic Retinopathy Screening in medical NCD clinics

Bring diabetic eye screening into the clinic where people with diabetes are already receiving care.

01

The missed eye screening

People with diabetes rarely have an eye examination in their routine medical care.

02

Put screening where patients already are

This opportunistic strategy places a trained and equipped mid-level eye care specialist in a medical NCD clinic.

03

A higher-risk

The patients attending these clinics are at higher risk of having all forms of eye disease including sight-threatening diabetic retinopathy.

It makes sense therefore to actively and opportunistically screen for diabetic retinopathy as well as other eye diseases in these clinics.

INTRODUCTION

Screening Diabetic Retinopathy and Other Eye Diseases

Diabetic retinopathy matters, but it is not the only common cause of treatable sight loss in these patients.

01

Look beyond diabetic retinopathy

Alongside diabetic retinopathy, these patients commonly have: refractive error, presbyopia, cataract, glaucoma

If screening focuses only on retinopathy, many treatable causes of sight loss can be missed.

  • Refractive error
  • Presbyopia
  • Cataract
  • Glaucoma
02

Fundoscopy is essential, but also...

Screening typically focuses on looking at the back of the eye by performing fundoscopy.

We will use the Arclight ophthalmoscope to examine the retina through a dilated pupil, but the screening should include more than this.

  1. Take a history
  2. Test visual acuity
  3. Check pupil reactions to light
  4. Examine the front of the eye
  5. Perform the fundal reflex
  6. Then dilate the pupils for fundoscopy
03

Identify more treatable causes of sight loss

These additional examinations help identify patients with:

  • Refractive error
  • Presbyopia
  • Cataract
  • Glaucoma

If screening focuses only on retinopathy, many treatable causes of sight loss can be missed.

WHAT IS DIABETES AND DIABETIC RETINOPATHY?

What is Diabetes?

Diabetes means the sugar level in the blood is too high.

01

Food becomes glucose

Food, especially carbohydrates, is broken down into sugar called glucose.

02

Insulin moves glucose into the body

Insulin is a hormone that helps move sugar from the blood into the body so it can be used for energy.

Food Glucose in blood Insulin opens the door Energy for the body
03

In diabetes, insulin is missing or not working

There is not enough insulin, or insulin does not work properly. Sugar stays in the blood instead of being used by the body.

04

High sugar damages
the body

Over time, high blood sugar damages nerves and blood vessels all over the body, including the eyes.

WHAT IS DIABETES AND DIABETIC RETINOPATHY?

What is Diabetic Retinopathy?

Diabetic retinopathy is damage to the retina caused by high sugar levels in the blood.

01

The retina lets you see

The retina is the light-sensitive layer at the back of the eye that allows vision.

Retina
02

Diabetic retinopathy

Diabetic retinopathy is damage to the retina caused by high sugar levels in the blood. High sugar damages the tiny blood vessels in the retina. This causes them to become narrowed and blocked. The retina becomes starved of oxygen which causes a hormone to be released (VEGF).

Blood vessels leak fluid and fat

The hormone causes blood vessels to leak fluid and fat damaging the central retina. This is maculopathy.

New weak blood vessels grow

The hormone also makes new weak blood vessels to grow. They bleed easily and cause scarring of the retina and sight loss.

04

Symptoms often come late

At first, there are no symptoms. Over time, diabetic retinopathy can cause:

  • Gradual blurred central vision
  • Dark spots or shadows
  • Sudden painless complete loss of vision
  • High pressure with painful red eye and vision loss

WHAT IS DIABETES AND DIABETIC RETINOPATHY?

Vision Loss in Diabetes

Vision loss mainly happens when normal vessels leak or when new fragile vessels bleed.

There are two main ways that loss of vision can happen in diabetes

High blood sugar
A Leaking normal vessels Fluid + hard exudates -> maculopathy
B New fragile vessels Bleeding + scarring + high pressure
Focus

Consider two aspects

When examining the fundus in patients with diabetes, consider:

1. Retina for retinopathy
2. Macula for maculopathy.

A

Normal blood vessels start to leak fluid

Fluid leaks into the retina, leaving yellow marks called hard exudates.

The macula is the central part of the retina. When it becomes swollen and fluid-filled, central vision becomes blurry.

This happens painlessly over weeks and months. Damage mainly affecting the macula is called maculopathy.

This is what maculopathy looks like on direct ophthalmoscopy with an Arclight. The pupils are dilated and the media is clear with no cataract or bleeding in the jelly of the eye.

Maculopathy on direct ophthalmoscopy - mild cortical cataract

B

New fragile blood vessels grow

These new vessels damage the eye in three main ways.

  • Bleeding onto the retina and into the vitreous
  • Scarring of the retina causing retinal detachment
  • Blocking fluid drainage causing rubeotic glaucoma

Causes of loss of vision

INTEGRATE INTO MEDICAL NCD CLINIC FLOW

Introduction

The approach deliberately looks for all major causes of avoidable blindness, while still identifying sight-threatening diabetic retinopathy and maculopathy.

01

Screen patients while they are at medical NCD clinic

Eye screening fits into routine non-communicable disease (NCD) care rather than waiting for a separate eye-clinic visit.

Arrival NCD care Eye screen Refer / help
02

Four checks inside the clinic flow

The pathway uses four practical checks:

  1. History of eye symptoms and diabetes
  2. Distance and near visual acuity with pinhole
  3. Loupe examination of pupils and front of eye
  4. Direct ophthalmoscopy for fundal reflex and fundus
03

Pragmatic, not perfect

The aim is not fine grading that can be achieved by retinal cameras. The aim is reliable detection of important problems.

Detect what changes management

The clinic screen should reliably detect:

  • Clear signs of proliferative disease
  • Maculopathy causing reduced vision
  • Media opacity, especially cataract or vitreous haemorrhage
  • Reduced vision from refractive error and presbyopia
  • Possible glaucoma

INTEGRATE INTO MEDICAL NCD CLINIC FLOW

Proliferative and Other Disease

These examples show sight-threatening diabetic eye disease and other common eye disease that should not be missed in NCD screening.

01

New vessels

New vessels on the disc, elsewhere, or on the iris are sight-threatening signs.

02

Bleeding

Bleeding on the retina or into the vitreous can cause sudden vision loss and needs referral.

03

Fibrovascular complications

Fibrovascular strands and tractional retinal detachment are advanced complications of proliferative disease.

04

Maculopathy

Maculopathy affects central vision and is sight-threatening when it reduces vision.

05

Sight Threatening Diabetic Eye Disease

These findings need referral because they can cause permanent sight loss.

06

Other eye disease

Other important eye disease includes cataract, need for glasses, and glaucoma.
