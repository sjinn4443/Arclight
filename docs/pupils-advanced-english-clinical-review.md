# Pupils Advanced: English clinical and language review

Reviewed on 6 November 2026. Scope: all 35 English narration cues, their scroll
and full-animation subtitles, section headings and visible diagram terminology.
This is a reference-based editorial review, not independent clinical sign-off.

## Clinical findings and edits

| Topic                                      | Decision                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Afferent pathway                           | Retained the retinal, optic nerve, chiasmal, tract and nuclear sequence. The light-reflex relay is in the pretectal midbrain, with bilateral input to the parasympathetic nuclei. |
| Efferent pathway                           | Removed the vague description as the “opposite” pathway. It carries signals from brain to eye.                                                                                    |
| Parasympathetic supply                     | Named the iris sphincter and clarified the ciliary muscle's focusing role.                                                                                                        |
| Third nerve and blinking                   | Specified **most** eye movements and lid elevation. Added that the facial nerve controls blinking, so the illustrated blink is not attributed to the third nerve.                 |
| Sympathetic supply                         | Named Müller's muscle and its contribution to upper lid elevation. Retained the sympathetic chain and superior cervical ganglion.                                                 |
| Direct and consensual responses            | Retained the distinction between the illuminated and fellow eye and the bilateral response to light.                                                                              |
| Observation                                | Replaced the unclear comparison with “eyelids” with a check for drooping lids.                                                                                                    |
| Pupil appearance and cataract              | Distinguished grey or white appearance from the ability to constrict. Retained the qualification that a cataractous eye can respond if the light-reflex pathway is intact.        |
| Illustrated retinal/optic nerve conditions | Clarified that the associations shown do not establish a diagnosis from pupil response alone.                                                                                     |
| Swinging light test                        | Added a two-to-three-second pause on each side. Normal responses are compared between the eyes.                                                                                   |
| RAPD                                       | Made the relative difference explicit: one eye sends a weaker signal than the other. Retained bilateral dilation on switching to the affected right eye in this example.          |
| Near response                              | Included convergence, pupil constriction and lens accommodation. Retained the advice to investigate abnormal convergence.                                                         |

## Sources

- [University of Texas: Ocular Motor System](https://nba.uth.tmc.edu/neuroscience/m/s3/chapter07.html): light-reflex circuitry, iris muscles, near responses and facial nerve supply for lid closure.
- [University of Michigan: Lid in Cross Section](https://public.websites.umich.edu/~aeyeskec/theeyeshaveit/anatomy/section-eyelid.html): sympathetic supply and the modest lifting action of Müller's muscle.
- [Community Eye Health: How to test for an RAPD](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5365042/): comparison between eyes and bilateral dilation when illumination switches to the affected side.
- [NSW Agency for Clinical Innovation: Eye Emergency Manual](https://aci.health.nsw.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0013/155011/ACI-Eye-Emergency-Manual.pdf): two-to-three-second illumination on each side.

## Language and delivery

Applied the humanizer skill after the clinical review: removed diagram commentary,
shortened the pathway explanation and kept direct instructions suitable for speech.
English uses British spelling, including “coloured”, “grey”, “towards” and
“inwards”. Lists have no Oxford comma. Appositive commas around “or RAPD” remain.

The shared PEC artwork and its original lesson are preserved. Three Advanced-only
English wording overrides replace the corresponding portions of reused PEC audio;
the remaining PEC recording is retained. English uses en-GB-SoniaNeural. Korean
recordings are retained. Word-boundary timing keeps the pupil response, eye movement
cue and warning icon aligned with the revised English recording.
